Course Intended Learning Outcomes (verbatim from syllabus)

CILOOutcome
CILO1Describe the innovations of South and West Asian cultures and civilisations and the ways in which their legacies manifest in Hong Kong in the present day.
CILO2Apply a multi-disciplinary perspective, including the methodologies of history of science and technology, anthropology, history, migration studies, the social sciences, cultural studies, and the visual arts, to understanding the global diffusion of Asian cultural heritage.
CILO3Analyse different types of source materials, including written texts, oral histories, multi-media, material objects and art, in order to explain historical and contemporary issues.
CILO4Evaluate processes of global cultural flow and creative cultural appropriation by applying examples from the Hong Kong context.

Recommended topic + structure

Topic recommendation

"Hybridity and Everyday Religion: The Role of Halal Food and Mosque Networks in Chungking Mansions as a Site of South Asian Diaspora in Hong Kong"

Why this works (drawn from L4 lecture + Week 3 + readings):

  • Chungking Mansions is the canonical "low-end globalisation" case study (L4) and the central case the syllabus calls out for Week 3-4.
  • Halal food is a tangible cultural expression linking religion + diaspora + everyday life — a perfect CILO1+4 fit.
  • Mosque networks (Jamia Mosque, Kowloon Masjid) provide a built-in field-site for segment 2.
  • The 5 concepts from Week 3 (cultural flow, diaspora, hybridity, everyday religion, colonial labour) all apply naturally.

Rubric breakdown

The syllabus text is brief on the precise rubric ("important academic concepts and perspectives, inter-disciplinary approach, as well as the application of a wide range of both primary and secondary sources will also be expected to show throughout the presentations"). Below is a sensible allocation of the 20%, based on the CILOs the presentation addresses (1-4) and the segment weightings the syllabus implies.

CriterionWeightWhat it means for this group
Concept application (5 concepts from Week 3)25%Each of the 5 concepts appears explicitly and is applied, not just named.
Content depth (food + mosque + Chungking Mansions)25%Three concrete sub-cases, each with detail — not generic claims.
3-segment structure (per syllabus)15%The analysis · field visit · reflection shape is clear and time-balanced.
Source mix (primary + secondary)15%Mathews book, Erni/Leung chapters, O'Connor chapter, field photos, newspaper articles.
Interdisciplinary approach10%At least 3 of the CILO2 disciplines show up (history, anthropology, cultural studies are easy wins).

The biggest single lever is concept application — if your slides make the 5 concepts feel inevitable to the story, you've already won a quarter of the marks.

Field site suggestions (for segment 2)

  • Kowloon Masjid & Islamic Centre — largest mosque in HK; Tsim Sha Tsui (right next to Chungking Mansions). Active community programs; rich photo ops.
  • Jamia Mosque — oldest mosque in HK (1849); central; relevant to Week 3 slide deck.
  • Gurdwara Sahib Hong Kong (Sikh Temple) — in Happy Valley; historic Sikh-trooper & colonial-labour connection (a Week-3 concept).
  • Chungking Mansions itself — walk-through, food court, guesthouses. Show the "low-end globalisation" theme from the L4 deck.
  • Zainab Al-Askari Mosque — a smaller mosque; good for showing range of Muslim communities in HK.
  • Indian/Pakistani/Nepali community associations (e.g. Indian Association of HK, Pakistani Association) — potential for interviews; advance permission needed.

AI-usage report (paste-able text)

Verbatim from the syllabus: "Proper application of AI assistance on the group presentation project is accepted. The students have to submit the report of AI assistance on group presentation project in order to clearly illustrate what kinds of AI tools have been applied, how did they apply the AI assistance on the group presentation project, and any fact-checking and revision phase have been taken throughout the making of group presentation project."

The declaration must include: which AI tools, how they were used, and what fact-checking / revision was done. Below is a working draft Regina can adapt:

Report of AI Assistance on Group Presentation Project
Group: [name] · Course: GTCU2027 · Date: ____

1. AI tools used
   - HKBU GenAI (gpt-4.1-mini) — via the university gateway.
   - [e.g. ChatGPT, Claude, Gemini — if the group uses anything else, list it].
   - [e.g. GitHub Copilot / Zed agent — for routine scaffolding only].

2. How AI was used
   - Generating the initial outline of the 40-minute presentation and aligning it with the 3-segment syllabus requirement.
   - Drafting slide bullets from annotated reading notes (Erni & Leung, O'Connor, Mathews).
   - Polishing the spoken script for clarity and pacing.
   - Summarising the course outline PDF and lecture content into a planning document.

3. Fact-checking and revision
   - Every AI-generated claim about historical fact was cross-referenced against the original reading (Mathews, Erni & Leung, O'Connor) and the lecture slides. No claim was taken from AI alone.
   - Every AI-generated citation was verified to exist in the actual converted-md / course-outline source.
   - All AI-generated slide bullets were rewritten by a group member to reflect our own interpretation.
   - The spoken script is human-drafted; AI was used only for phrasing polish, not for content invention.

4. What AI was NOT used for
   - The spoken word of any presenter (this must be human-authored).
   - The conclusion / reflection segment — this is the CILO4 component and is human-authored.
   - Any factual claim about Chungking Mansions, halal food, or mosque history that we could not verify in the source readings.
