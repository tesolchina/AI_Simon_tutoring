Recommended presentation structure (the spine)

A 25-min talk is short. Each concept = one slide; each case study = 1–2 slides.

  1. Hook (1 slide, 1 min). Chungking Mansions "17 storeys, 140+ shops" frame, OR Jamia Mosque "oldest mosque in HK, 1849" frame.
  2. Central question (1 slide, 30 sec). "How does everyday inter-Asian contact invent the cultures we now call 'Hong Kong'?"
  3. The 5 concepts in 5 slides (~1 min each).
  4. The case study (3–4 slides, ~5 min). Chungking Mansions — one Mathews pull-quote per concept.
  5. Pre-history vs. present (2 slides, ~3 min). Colonial labour → today's South Asian communities.
  6. What we leave out / Q&A seeds (1 slide, 30 sec). 3 things you didn't cover.
  7. Closing (1 slide, 30 sec). Tie back to central question.

Suggested declaration text (paste and adapt)

AI-usage declaration

We used AI tools (specifically: HKBU GenAI for outlining, Google Drive
built-in OCR + pymupdf-blocks for material conversion, and a personal
agent for materials indexing) for the following clearly bounded tasks:
  (a) converting PDF readings into searchable markdown so we could quote them,
  (b) drafting an outline from the materials inventory, and
  (c) generating first-draft slide bullets from annotated notes.

Every AI-generated artefact was reviewed and edited by the named human
authors. AI did not write the spoken script, the conclusions, or any
analytical claims that we could not cite back to a reading. All claims
about historical fact, scholarly argument, or the Chungking Mansions
case study are sourced from the listed readings. Where AI was used for
language polishing, the underlying thinking is our own.

Tools used (with versions, where applicable):
  - Google Drive built-in PDF export
  - PyMuPDF (pymupdf-blocks mode)
  - pdftotext (poppler)
  - Tesseract OCR
  - HKBU GenAI (gpt-4.1-mini) — accessed through the university gateway
  - GitHub Copilot / Zed agent (used for routine scaffolding, not for substantive claims)

Limitations acknowledged:
  - The course outline PDF was not OCR'd by us at preparation time, so
    parts of this plan are marked "best guess" pending confirmation
    from Alfred Lam.
  - All readings are in English except two Chinese-language reviews of
    Mathews; we relied on the existing English summary in the index
    rather than re-reading those in Chinese.
